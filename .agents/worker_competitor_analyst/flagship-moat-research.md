# Evidence-Gated Flagship Workflow Research

Find the **best credible flagship workflow**, not a random feature list. Show the full credible candidate set, including high-demand, lower-value utilities, then select the strongest strategic recommendation or return **no flagship now**.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

1. Treat a statement as fact only when a source supports it. Use **Verified**, **Derived**, **Inference**, or **Hypothesis** labels from [the evidence protocol](references/evidence-protocol.md).
2. Never invent competitor names, pricing, plan limits, complaints, traffic, search demand, user behavior, technical capability, or outcome metrics.
3. Do not translate “not found” into “competitor lacks it.” Mark the status **Unknown** unless a tested workflow or first-party evidence establishes absence.
4. Do not mistake “free,” “AI,” “local,” “faster,” or “more features” for a moat. Explain the mechanism that creates user value and the easiest way it could be copied.
5. Do not force a flagship. A **no-go** is correct when evidence, quality feasibility, or buyer-choice proof is insufficient.
6. Include all candidates that survive the minimum evidence bar. A moderate-value but high-demand candidate is still a candidate; classify it as a **growth utility** if it is not a flagship.
7. Preserve the user’s stated constraints. Do not recommend changes that violate approved architecture, privacy, UX, pricing, regulatory, or deployment boundaries.

## Intake and Scope

Before research, establish the following from user-provided documentation, the repository, the live product, and questions only when a material answer is missing:

| Required input | Establish |
|---|---|
| Product baseline | Product purpose, real target user, live and documented capabilities, user flow, current quality limits, pricing, and claims that are only aspirational. |
| Constraints | Architecture, budget, processing/privacy boundaries, UI/UX limits, timeline, team capability, and prohibited changes. |
| Research universe | Market/country, direct competitors, adjacent products, substitutes, and user-authorized competitors or data sources. |
| Decision objective | Flagship workflow, growth utility, monetizable moat, or all three; define the time horizon and success condition. |

If documentation and the live product disagree, state the discrepancy and use **live verified behavior** as the product claim boundary.

## Workflow

### 1. Build the Current-Product Baseline

Inventory the real user workflow from entry to outcome. Separate capabilities into **live verified**, **documented but unverified**, **in development**, and **not present**. Identify where users spend time, lose confidence, repeat manual work, or abandon the flow; do not invent user analytics or complaints.

Write one precise job-to-be-done statement before examining solutions. Avoid broad labels such as “AI editor” or “productivity app.”

### 2. Map the Competitive and Substitute Workflow

Research direct competitors, adjacent products, manual alternatives, and “do nothing” substitutes. For each relevant option, collect first-party product, pricing, documentation, and help-center evidence before using third-party commentary.

Map the actual workflow—not only the feature checklist. Capture:

| Boundary | Record |
|---|---|
| Access | Login, installation, device, upload, setup, data handling, and collaboration dependencies. |
| Job completion | Inputs, steps, review/control, output, export/handoff, and retry/restore behavior. |
| Commercial boundary | Free limit, watermark, usage cap, paid tier, add-on, or unknown. |
| Quality boundary | Stated limitations, confidence controls, error handling, validation, and recovery. |
| Evidence state | Verified available, verified limited/paid, verified absent after testing, or unknown. |

Use reviews, forums, and social posts only as attributed directional evidence. Do not generalize a single complaint into market-wide demand.

### 3. Find Problems and Generate Candidates Broadly

Look for recurring, painful, valuable problems through: missing capabilities, fragmented workflows, paid/limited capabilities, manual steps, quality/review gaps, underserved segments, unserved handoffs, and product-data/feedback loops.

Generate candidates in at least these classes when evidence exists:

1. **Flagship workflow candidate:** A connected outcome that improves completion, quality, trust, or control in a meaningful way.
2. **High-demand growth utility:** A simpler or lower-value capability with credible demand, even if it is easier to imitate.
3. **Paid-to-free wedge:** A capability users pay for or face a verified limit on, where free/cheaper delivery is technically and economically credible.
4. **Workflow unifier:** A practical sequence that replaces manual hopping across multiple tools.
5. **Underserved segment candidate:** A solution tailored to an evidenced group with a distinct unmet need.

Do not discard a candidate merely because it is not a moat. Classify it accurately. Do not elevate a high-search-demand tool to flagship status without a non-price buyer-choice reason.

### 4. Screen, Score, and Rank Candidates

Use the rubric in [the opportunity scorecard](references/opportunity-scorecard.md). Score every serious candidate across the eight required dimensions. Treat scores as transparent decision aids, not measured facts; cite the evidence and confidence behind each score.

Apply these tests to each candidate:

- **Problem test:** What precise user problem is solved, at what workflow moment, and why does it matter?
- **Demand test:** What source establishes demand? Distinguish search demand, stated pain, purchase behavior, and inference.
- **Buyer-choice test:** Why would a user select this product over a named existing option, beyond being free or cheaper?
- **Workflow test:** Show the end-to-end before/after journey, including inputs, decisions, control, output, and recovery.
- **Quality test:** What can fail? What validation, preview, confidence signal, or decline path protects users?
- **Copy test:** Describe the fastest credible competitor copy path and what, if anything, makes the advantage durable.
- **Economics test:** Explain the likely cost/resource boundary. Do not call a feature “free” or “unlimited” unless its delivery model supports that claim.
- **Fit test:** Confirm alignment with the product’s target user, architecture, distribution, and constraints.

### 5. Run Mandatory Final Verification

Before a verdict, run the two independent reviews and all delivery gates in [the final-verification protocol](references/final-verification-protocol.md). Require an **Evidence Auditor** to challenge factual/source integrity and an **Adversarial Strategy Reviewer** to challenge candidate completeness, buyer choice, workflow quality, score logic, copy risk, and the no-go decision.

When independent agent delegation is unavailable, run two independently scoped review passes and disclose this accurately. Do not state that two agents verified the result unless two separate agents actually completed the reviews. Resolve every material blocker before continuing; otherwise downgrade the decision to **research candidate** or **no flagship now**.

### 6. Decide and Explain

Use the required report structure in [the output template](references/output-template.md). Present **every credible candidate** in a full opportunity table and give each one a short dedicated explanation.

Then select one of the following outcomes:

| Outcome | Use when |
|---|---|
| **Recommended flagship workflow** | A candidate has a serious user problem, defensible buyer choice, feasible quality path, and adequate evidence. |
| **Growth utility, not flagship** | Demand is credible, but differentiation or moat potential is weak. Explain its acquisition role and copy risk. |
| **Research candidate** | The problem is plausible but key demand, competitor, quality, or cost evidence is missing. State the exact validation needed. |
| **No flagship now** | No candidate clears the evidence and quality gates. Recommend only the research/quality work needed to reassess. |

## Required Final Standard

For the selected flagship, provide an end-to-end workflow specification, target user, alternatives compared, buyer-choice proof, moat/copy-risk analysis, scope boundary, quality gates, validation plan, and explicit unknowns. For all lower-ranked candidates, explain what they are, why they help or fail, and why they rank lower.

Never hide uncertainty in a confident narrative. If a source does not prove a claim, say so.
