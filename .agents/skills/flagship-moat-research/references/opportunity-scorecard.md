# Opportunity Scorecard

Use this rubric to compare serious candidates. Scores are **decision aids**, not market facts. Attach a short evidence rationale and confidence level to every score.

## Scoring Scale

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Weak, unsupported, materially misaligned, or likely harmful. |
| 2 | Limited evidence or value; important blockers remain. |
| 3 | Plausible and useful with mixed evidence or meaningful trade-offs. |
| 4 | Strong evidence and a credible strategic advantage. |
| 5 | Exceptional evidence and advantage relative to the current product, user, and market. |

Use **N/A** only when a dimension genuinely cannot apply. Explain why. Never assign a high score just because information is missing.

## Required Dimensions

| Dimension | Question | Score 1 | Score 3 | Score 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem Severity** | How painful, frequent, risky, costly, or time-consuming is the exact user problem? | Cosmetic/convenience only or no evidence. | Useful recurring friction for a defined segment. | Material outcome, revenue, time, trust, or failure risk for a clearly evidenced segment. |
| **Demand Evidence** | How strong and direct is the evidence that users seek or need it? | No evidence beyond intuition. | Some dated demand signals or multiple directional signals. | Strong, current, market-specific data and/or repeated direct user evidence. |
| **Competitive Gap** | What is meaningfully missing, limited, fragmented, or poorly executed today? | Existing workflows already solve it well; no gap established. | A real limitation exists, but alternatives address much of it. | A verified, consequential gap across relevant alternatives. |
| **Differentiation** | Is the proposed outcome meaningfully better for a user than named alternatives? | A feature checklist or price-only difference. | Noticeable workflow or control improvement. | Clear before/after outcome, trustworthy quality/control, and a non-price reason to switch. |
| **Moat Potential** | What could make the advantage durable? | Single toggle; easy copy. | Workflow integration, distribution, or implementation quality offers temporary advantage. | Strong compounding workflow, trust/data/proof loop, switching friction, or defensible distribution. |
| **Build Feasibility** | Can the current product/team build and validate it safely within stated constraints? | Requires incompatible infrastructure, unknown research breakthrough, or unacceptable cost. | Feasible with material technical/product risk. | Fits current architecture/capabilities with bounded risks and clear validation. |
| **Monetization Potential** | Does it create credible revenue, retention, expansion, or strategic value? | No credible value path. | Could support retention, upsell, or indirect acquisition. | Evidenced willingness-to-pay, high-value workflow, or clear durable business leverage. |
| **Strategic Fit** | Does it reinforce the product’s users, positioning, distribution, and constraints? | Distracting adjacent product or constraint conflict. | Helpful but expands scope or positioning. | Directly reinforces the core product and a credible path to distribution. |

## Confidence and Evidence Weight

Give each candidate an evidence confidence: **High**, **Medium**, or **Low**.

| Confidence | Standard |
|---|---|
| **High** | Most material claims are verified through current primary sources, live tests, authorized internal data, or reproducible datasets. |
| **Medium** | Core problem and product comparison are supported, but one or more major dimensions depend on inference or directional data. |
| **Low** | Candidate depends mainly on hypotheses, incomplete competitor coverage, unavailable demand data, or untested technical feasibility. |

A high numerical score with low confidence is a **research candidate**, not a launch recommendation.

## Candidate Classes

Classify each candidate after scoring. Do not use total score alone.

| Class | Typical pattern | Required interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Flagship workflow** | Strong problem, demand, differentiation, feasibility, and strategic fit; at least a plausible non-price buyer-choice argument. | Recommend only with an explicit end-to-end workflow and quality-validation plan. |
| **High-demand growth utility** | Strong demand and feasible build, but modest severity, moat, or differentiation. | Include it fully; explain acquisition value, likely copy risk, and why it is not the flagship. |
| **Paid-to-free wedge** | Verified paid/limited boundary and a credible low-cost/free delivery path. | Explain why free is economically feasible and what differentiates beyond price. |
| **Workflow unifier** | Multiple verified manual or fragmented steps can be connected. | Explain each displaced handoff and the observable outcome improvement. |
| **Underserved segment bet** | Strong segment-specific pain with incomplete broad-market evidence. | Specify the segment, direct evidence, and research needed before scaling. |
| **Research candidate** | Promising but a key demand, quality, cost, or competitor fact is unknown. | Provide the exact next validation, not a fabricated roadmap. |
| **Reject/deprioritize** | Weak demand, already well solved, low fit, unsafe build, or no meaningful user outcome. | Keep it in the final report with a concise rejection reason. |

## Selection Logic

Use the following rules in order:

1. **Keep broad coverage.** Include every candidate that has a defined problem, feasible user workflow, and at least one material evidence signal. A high-demand candidate does not need exceptional problem severity to be reported.
2. **Reject false certainty.** Do not rank unknown competitor absence, untested demand, or assumed technical quality as an advantage.
3. **Choose the flagship on outcome, not total score alone.** A strong flagship must have a non-price buyer-choice proof, quality feasibility, strategic fit, and a credible response to the copy test.
4. **Keep acquisition and moat distinct.** A high-demand utility may be a better traffic/acquisition candidate than the flagship. Say so explicitly.
5. **Allow two-track strategy only when justified.** Recommend one flagship plus one growth utility only if they reinforce the same product story and do not dilute delivery quality. Otherwise select one priority.
6. **Use no-go correctly.** If no candidate has credible buyer-choice proof or quality feasibility, return “no flagship now,” even if several high-demand utilities exist.

## Candidate Worksheet

Use this complete worksheet for every serious candidate.

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Candidate name and class | Plain-language name; provisional class. |
| What it is | Concise description of the user-visible capability or connected workflow. |
| User and trigger | Target user, job-to-be-done, and the moment they need it. |
| Problem and current workaround | Exact pain, current steps/tools, and consequences. |
| Evidence | Claim IDs/source references and evidence labels. |
| Competitor boundary | Named alternatives; available, limited/paid, absent-after-test, not advertised, or unknown. |
| Proposed before/after workflow | Input → processing/decision → review/control → output → recovery. |
| Buyer-choice proof | Why use this instead of a named alternative; non-price reason required for flagship. |
| Quality and cost boundary | Failure modes, validation/decline design, dependencies, and credible economics. |
| Copy risk / durable advantage | Fastest copy path and workflow/data/distribution/trust advantage, if any. |
| Scores and confidence | Eight scores, cited rationale, and High/Medium/Low confidence. |
| Decision | Flagship, growth utility, research candidate, or reject; exact reason. |
