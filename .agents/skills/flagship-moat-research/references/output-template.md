### Candidate [rank]: [Name]

**What it is.** Explain the actual user-visible workflow in plain language. Name the trigger, input, decisions/review, output, and recovery/undo path.

**Problem and demand.** Explain why the problem matters and cite the narrow evidence. Distinguish verified demand from inference. If demand is high but user value is modest, say so; describe its acquisition role and why it remains a candidate.

**Competitive position.** Compare only verified workflow boundaries against named alternatives. State missing evidence as unknown.

**Why it could win or fail.** Describe buyer choice, non-price differentiation, quality/cost constraints, fastest copy path, and evidence confidence. State why the candidate ranks where it does.

Repeat for each credible candidate. Keep rejected ideas in a brief appendix when they were seriously considered.

### 6.4 Quality, Cost, and Risk Gates

State concrete go/no-go tests before implementation. Include failure modes, user review/restore requirements, benchmark or ground-truth need, privacy/cost limits, and unsupported claims that cannot be marketed yet.

### 6.5 Validation Plan

Describe the smallest credible research/prototype/test needed to validate demand, quality, buyer choice, and economics. Do not fabricate a timeline, test result, or KPI baseline.

## 7. Alternatives That Rank Lower

List the 2–3 strongest alternatives. For each, explain what it solves, why it remains valuable, and the specific reason it ranks lower: weaker buyer choice, easier copying, demand uncertainty, feasibility risk, lower strategic fit, or insufficient quality evidence.

## 8. Explicit Unknowns and No-Claim Zone

List every material unresolved question and the exact evidence needed. State claims that must not be made until verified—for example “first,” “only,” “unlimited,” “free forever,” “lossless,” “privacy-preserving,” compatibility, accuracy, market demand, or competitor absence.
